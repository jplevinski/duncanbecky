Chapter 7: The Ragged Goblin

Part 1

QUEST COMPLETE: EARN YOUR KEEP
Rewards: 50 Credits, 200 XP, 1× Mystery Spud Juice
Warning: Drink at your own peril. Induces Severely Intoxicated, rendering the user immune to damage for two minutes—at the cost of a dangerously steep rise in BAC.

They paid off their bar tabs without ceremony.

Becky immediately purchased access to the showers. Duncan, meanwhile, ordered another bottle of Dragon's Piss.

The shower was incredible.

Hot water blasted away goblin filth, sweat, blood, and whatever else Greater Mellonwood had decided to smear on Becky's existence. Her clothes, however, were beyond salvation. Vern wordlessly handed her a white cloth robe instead.

It was surprisingly soft. A yellow rope cinched it at her waist, shaping the fabric just enough to feel intentional. With the robe draped over her frame and her staff resting against her shoulder, she looked—against all odds—downright wizardly.

Duncan watched her pass, dripping clean and wrapped in dignity.

Then he turned back to the bar.

He could shower later.

Right now, he needed another bottle of Dragon's Piss.

He needed the spike.

So he downed it.

Then another.

Then another.

The breathalyzer chimed.

Duncan blew into it. 0.231.

That was enough to prep for the next misadventure—or possibly too much. Either way, it would have to do.

He showered after that.

Clean water sluiced goblin grime, sweat, and shame down the drain. When he emerged, he was dressed in what passed for medieval peasant attire: rough pants, a scratchy shirt, functional but ugly and miserable.

"Of course," Duncan muttered, tugging at the fabric. "She gets the good-looking robe. I get scrubs."

The AI didn't respond.

Duncan didn't wait for it to respond. He just headed back to the bar, where Becky was sipping a martini and chatting easily with Vern.

"Duncan, my friend," Vern said warmly. "You've cleaned up well." He slid another drink across the counter. "On the house."

Becky turned, giving him a once-over. "Not terrible," she admitted. "Grease all washed off and in clothes that actually fit."

"You think these fit?" Duncan scratched at his sleeve. "They itch everywhere."

"Poor-quality materials," Becky said, popping a green olive into her mouth. She chewed, then added, "Vern was telling me about professions."

Vern perked up instantly. "Ah—yes. Let me take it from here."

He pulled out his phone. "It's this app right here," without a hint of irony, it was named PROFESSIONS. Becky and Duncan followed along on their own phones.

"You get one profession immediately," he explained. "At level twenty, you unlock a second. At forty, a third."

"So what do they actually do?" Duncan asked.

"They function like a job system," Vern said, "but they're tied directly to your class."

He reached for Becky's phone. "Since Becky's a Priest, she's got three starting options. Same rule always applies—three choices at a time. They'll change at higher levels."

Becky leaned in, trying to follow along with the words as Vern read them outloud.

PALADIN
Trades magical potency for heavy armor, melee combat, and weapon buffs. Healing remains, but at reduced strength. Excels at protection and damage mitigation.

HEALER
Doubles down on support. Maximum healing efficiency and party buffs. Loses all offensive spells except Smite. Best pure healer in the game.

SHADOW CASTER
Abandons most holy magic in exchange for shadow knowledge. Limited healing for others, but strong self-sustain and high damage output.

"Ohh… choices, choices."

Becky leaned closer to the screen, visibly delighted. Against all odds, she was starting to enjoy the RPG parts of this nightmare.

Duncan pulled up his own options.

BERSERKER
Abandons defense for raw, reckless offense. Converts Rage directly into damage, attack speed, and temporary health. Armor effectiveness drops. Blocking becomes unreliable. Excels at ending fights fast—or dying spectacularly trying.

BATTLE GUARD
Fully commits to protection and control. Gains enhanced shield techniques, taunts, damage reduction, and positioning skills. Damage output is lower, but enemies struggle to reach allies while Duncan stands. The most stable option—if he can stay upright.

WRECKER
Specializes in environmental destruction and crowd disruption. Gains bonuses to knockdowns, charges, terrain damage, and improvised weapons. Less precise, brutally effective. Thrives when everything is already going wrong.

"Now," Vern began, "you two will want to coordinate. Pick professions that compli—"

"Shadow Caster!"

Becky tapped the option without hesitation.

ACHIEVEMENT UNLOCKED: PICK YOUR PROFESSION
Welcome, Shadow Caster.
You are trading survivability for pure destruction.
Statistical note: what a Becky move.

Her robes shifted instantly—from pristine white to deep, bruised purple.

SPELL UNLOCKED: DRAIN LIFE
Channel dark magic to siphon enemy HP and convert it into your own.

Vern winced. "That… removes a lot of your safety net."

Duncan scrolled through his choices again. Vern tried once more.

"That really leaves with one viable path, Duncan. Battle Guard would pair best—Becky's limited healing could keep you alive while she melts faces—"

"Wrecker," Duncan said.

He selected it.

ACHIEVEMENT UNLOCKED: PICK YOUR PROFESSION
Welcome, Wrecker.
Your femur appears fully healed.
Recommendation: apply former football instincts liberally.

Vern closed his eyes and sighed. "Unwise move Duncan."

Duncan shrugged. "I don't like standing still."

"Now you'll have to control crowds and end fights quickly," Vern said. "Because she won't be able to keep you alive."

Becky flexed her fingers, purple energy curling between them.

"Good," she said. "I don't want to."

Part 2

"Hey—maybe I'm just sloshed," Duncan said, squinting, "but did you do something here, Vern?"

The walls didn't glow anymore.

The matte sheen of cardboard was gone, replaced by something darker—uneven, cold-looking. Real.

"No, sir," Vern said evenly.

Duncan pushed himself up from the bar, swayed, and staggered toward the wall. He pressed his palm against it.

Stone.

Cool. Rough. It didn't give.

His stomach tightened.

"Becky," he said, a little quieter now. "Touch the wall."

She shot him a look. "You're getting hyped over walls now?"

"Just—please."

Becky leaned closer, squinting, then reached out. Her fingers stopped.

"…Oh."

She rapped her knuckles against it once. Hard.

Stone.

The room felt less like a toddler's art project and more like an actual medieval tavern.

Duncan glanced down at his waist.

The Fanny Pack of Childhood Memories was gone.

In its place hung a worn leather satchel, stitched tight and faintly glowing at the seams—magical, but practical. It rested against his hip with real weight.

His stomach gave a small, uneasy spike.

Duncan pushed through the door and stepped outside.

Mellonwood had changed.

The dirt paths were gone, replaced by uneven cobblestone. The buildings weren't cardboard facades anymore—they were timber and stone, dark with age. Even the banner had been replaced: a carved wooden sign, its lettering sharp and white, etched in a severe gothic script.

It felt like being shoved out of a perverted Yoshi's Woolly World and dumped straight into a darker The Witcher.

Duncan swallowed. "Thank the gods. Becky—everything changed. Come out here, you h—"

He stopped.

The word sat right there on his tongue, familiar and easy. Normally he'd have thrown it without thinking. Instead, something snagged in his chest. The goblins. The way she'd flinched. The way the world suddenly felt heavier.

"…you wonderful, uh—face-melting, badass," he finished lamely.

"You what?" Becky shouted from inside Vern's.

"Just—come look."

She stepped out and slowly turned in place, taking in the stone, the timber, the darkened sky. Her nose wrinkled.

"Holy shit," she said. "This is… dark. I liked how it looked before."

"You would," Duncan muttered, shaking his head.

He didn't get it. How could anyone miss the cardboard? This—this—was real now. Solid. Built to last. A world with weight, not some soft, forgiving playground slapped together by someone who refused to grow up.

Part 3

A shuffling sound carried from the alley.

Duncan turned, half-expecting another naked horror.

The goblin that stepped into view wasn't flailing anything.

It was fully clothed—layered, even. A stained tunic over worn undershirts, leather wraps around its arms, boots held together with mismatched straps. It looked cold. Tired.

Level 5 — Ragged Scout Goblin

"Psst. You two," the goblin said quietly. "I've got a proposition."

"Nope." Becky didn't hesitate. She spun on her heel and headed straight back into Vern's. "Absolutely not."

"What's her problem?" the goblin asked.

Duncan swallowed. "Uh… you might've said something she doesn't like."

He drew his sword.

Then reached for his shield.

ERROR.
HEY. YOU PICKED WRECKER.
WRECKERS DON'T USE SHIELDS.
ALSO, THAT SWORD'S A LITTLE DAINTY. THINK HEAVIER.

Duncan's grip tightened as he took a step forward.

The goblin immediately raised both hands and backed away.

"Whoa. Put that away," it said. "You're acting just like some of my kin—always waving their things around."

It shook its head.

"I'm not here to fight. I'm not a bad guy."

"What do you want?" Duncan said, sliding his sword back into its sheath.

The goblin exhaled, shoulders loosening just a fraction. "Like I said—I've got a proposition."

He reached into his satchel and produced a small glass vial, the liquid inside glowing a steady, reassuring red. He tossed it underhand toward Duncan.

"Minor Healing Potion," the goblin said. "Drink it, you get half your health back. No tricks."

Duncan caught it, turning the vial slowly in his fingers. "And how do I know it's not poison?"

WAY TO USE THAT LIMITED WISDOM.

The goblin snorted. "You don't. But you can ask Vern. I keep him stocked. He's one of my best customers."

Duncan frowned and tried to hand the potion back.

"Keep it," the goblin said, shaking his head. "My treat."

He hesitated, then added, more carefully now, "But I do need something from you."

"What kind of something?"

The goblin's grip tightened on his satchel.

"Materials."

Duncan glanced back toward Vern's bar, trying to catch Becky's expression—any sign of what she thought about this goblin.

Becky, meanwhile, was already laughing with Vern, martini in hand, hands slick with finger food, like none of this mattered.

"You see," the goblin said calmly, "our Life Berry supply has been corrupted. We're still tracking the cause. Some of my kind made… poor choices to survive."

Duncan thought of the three goblins he'd destroyed—their obscene bravado, their shattered bodies. His life had been in danger. He'd had no choice.

…Right?

It was just a quest.

But did that make it necessary? Or just easy?

"Oh," Duncan said quietly. "I see."

"We know you killed three of our youths," the goblin continued, without accusation. "Our elders warned them not to harass Mellonwood's citizens. Perhaps this will serve as a lesson."

The goblin folded its hands.

"Help me gather untainted Life Berries," it said. "We'll split the cache. Twenty percent to you."
