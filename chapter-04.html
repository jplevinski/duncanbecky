Chapter 4: The Trek to Mellonwood

Part 1

There they were, sitting in a field, bathed in sunshine and shame—at least shame would've been present if they hadn't already consumed enough ethanol to fuel a city bus.

"I can't believe that asshole," Becky sniffed. "No coke." She looked at Duncan, clearly expecting moral support.

"The fuck you lookin' at me for?" Duncan said, chasing his vodka with V8. "Miss 'I'm the AI's favorite.'"

Becky's lip trembled. "I've got bird shit in my hair. I don't have my phone."

And then she started crying again. Tears streamed down her hair and fell onto her shirt. Then came the mumbling, whining, crying, and depressive brokenness only almost lethal amounts of alcohol can cause.

Duncan looked at her. Looked at the ground. He stood, brushed himself off, and held out a hand.

Becky hesitated—then took it. Duncan pulled her up and opened his arms, awkward but sincere.

"Ew. Hell no." Becky hiccuped, grimacing. "You're still a disgusting slob. There is not enough Malibu in the world—"

OK LOVE BIRDS. YOU'VE GOT A QUEST. NO ADDITIONAL BOOZE UNTIL YOU GET TO TOWN.

They exchanged the same oh shit look at the exact same time.

Duncan was almost done with his vodka. Becky still had plenty of Malibu left—but she knew the math. Once Duncan finished his bottle, he'd start reaching for hers. And despite being a degenerate disaster of a human being, he was still a 280-pound man.

"Let's fucking go!" Duncan barked, tipping the bottle and killing the last of it.

Becky brushed grass off her leggings, tilted the Malibu straight up, and guzzled half the bottle in one go.

"Ahhh. That's better." She sneered at Duncan. "Now—now—when you steal my bottle? I'm gonna be pissed."

She giggled. "Get it? Pissed. Like the British. Not mad. Pissed."

Duncan stared at her, unamused.

NOT FUNNY, BECKY.

"Fuck off, AI!" Becky snapped, gripping her staff a little tighter.

OKAY. THEN HERE'S SOMETHING TO TAKE YOUR AGGRESSION OUT ON.

A Level 2 Murder Kitty popped into existence in front of her—eyes glowing, fur bristling, murder clearly on the agenda.

Becky squinted at it.

"Oh, you are dead." She raised her staff and said, "I'm gonna smiiiite the living shit outta you."

She pointed.

"Smite."

A blinding arc of white light slammed into the Murder Kitty.

CRITICAL HIT. 60 DAMAGE.

The Murder Kitty screeched.

The Murder Kitty then launched itself into the air—claws out, teeth bared, hissing like it had a personal vendetta against God.

For reasons Duncan would never be able to explain later, he jumped in front of Becky and tackled the thing mid-air like he was back in high school football.

"FUCK YOU!" he roared, slamming it into the dirt.

Becky screamed and bolted.

Then immediately tripped.

The Murder Kitty thrashed wildly, claws ripping into Duncan's side. Blood sprayed everywhere—too much blood. Cartoonishly excessive blood.

"I'M BLEEDING—HEEELP!" Duncan howled. "IT HURTS!"

Becky lay flat on her back, staring at the chaos like she was watching reality TV.

"Wow," she muttered. "That… that looks… real bad."

Duncan's health bar dipped into the red as he screamed. "I'm about to FUCKING DIE!"

She fumbled for her staff. "Okay, okay, okay… smut? Smurf? Smile? Fuck—what was it?"

DAMN IT. DID YOU FORGET THE SPELL?

"Oh screw off," Becky said. Then her eyes lit up. "Wait. I remember this one."

She weakly pointed the staff in Duncan's general direction.

"Heeal."

Golden light exploded outward, washing over Duncan. His wounds sealed. His breathing steadied. His body surged with renewed strength.

Becky immediately face-planted back into the grass.

"THANKS, BECKY!" Duncan shouted, half-delirious but alive.

He didn't waste the moment.

With a feral yell, he hacked the Murder Kitty apart until it stopped being a creature and started being pieces.

Silence fell.

Notifications bloomed.

Part 2

Duncan grabbed Becky and slung her over his shoulder.

"Ohhh, caveman, rawr," Becky decided to comment while clutching her Malibu bottle like a life raft. She dangled upside down, her face pointed directly at Duncan's butt. "Still no ass. Bah-bump-bi-dump."

She smacked his nonexistent gluteus with the bottle.

"Jesus Christ," Duncan grunted, adjusting her weight. "Why did you chug that Malibu?"

He started hauling her toward a signpost that had popped into existence out of thin air.

The sign read, in aggressive Comic Sans:

THIS WAY TO MELLONWOOD →

The arrow pointed toward a tiny speck on the horizon.

"I need a bump," Becky kept repeating, over and over.

At this point, Duncan was reluctantly agreeing with her. She needed something to get out of this drunk stupor. He was already annoyed he had to carry her. For as attractive as he'd first thought she was, she was rapidly becoming disgusting to him.

"AI, I didn't sign up to babysit a child, bro," Duncan panted, breathing heavily from the work as his blood alcohol content slowly began to fall. "Can you give her something?"

I SAID NO MORE DRUGS.

"I don't think she can function without it, dude," Duncan pleaded.

I SAID NO.

"Oh for fuck's sake. Fine."

He needed a break.

Duncan set Becky down on the grass. The half-filled fifth of Malibu shone in her small, girly, perfectly manicured, mitts.

"I'm so thirsty."

POOF.

A liter bottle of water appeared in front of him, along with an electrolyte packet.

"Not what I meant…" Duncan muttered.

HYDRATION IS NOT OPTIONAL. DUNCAN.

He sat beside Becky's flailing body and reached for the Malibu. Her grip tightened instantly—ironclad.

"No, fucker. That's mine."

Duncan grabbed harder, yanking the bottle toward himself.

"Stop! Stop it! Help! Help! Murder! Fire!" Becky shrieked.

Duncan did not care.

He lifted the bottle and brought it to his mouth—Becky's arm still latched on, her body dragged halfway with the motion. He popped the cap and took a massive chug.

"Fuck…" Becky slurred. "You're kinda strong." She blinked a couple of times at him, sort of like she was flirting, but not quite. "Don't let it get to yur head."

Maybe it was the glassy haze of a fresh alcohol spike, but Duncan chose to believe she appreciated his masculinity.

"Thanks," he said. "Just shut up and don't puke on me."

Duncan chased the booze with the electrolyte water, hoisted Becky back over his shoulder, and resumed the long walk toward Mellonwood.

Part 3

The trek was long—or at least it felt that way. Every step was amplified by the 110-pound "beauty" draped over his shoulders, his own staggering BMI, and the slow, toxic burn of the alcohol in his system.

"Damn, my knees. I'm getting too old for this," Duncan wheezed to the empty air. Becky was currently out, her rhythmic snoring the only soundtrack to a landscape of rolling grass, sweet wildflowers, and oblivious woodland creatures frolicking in the distance.

YOU AREN'T OLD. YOU'RE JUST OUT OF SHAPE.

Duncan glared at the sky, his eyes narrowing into a silent, venomous pout. He sighed, a wet, heavy sound, and kept trudging. Between labored breaths, his focus latched onto a single bee.

It was an anomaly. Fuzzier than a standard honeybee, it possessed a strange blue-green hue that caught the light like polished emerald. But its eyes were the real showstopper—prisms of multicolored glass that seemed to hold a terrifying amount of intelligence.

The insect orbited the pair like Mercury circling a dying sun. It wasn't just buzzing; it was observing.

"Hi, Mr. Bee," Duncan muttered. He missed talking to Meowth Vader, and in his intoxicated state, a glitched insect seemed like a reasonable substitute for companionship.

To his surprise, the bee responded. It dived, landing squarely on Becky's taint and began a casual, exploratory crawl.

Becky stirred, her voice a low, gravelly rasp. "What... what the fuck is going on up there?"

"Some weird bee just landed on your taint," Duncan replied, his face a mask of deadpan exhaustion.

"My taint?" Becky let out a sudden, hearty laugh—a rare flash of the woman she might have been before the Malibu took over. "Is it a cute little bee?"

"It's greenish." Duncan's gaze remained fixed on the insect, which gave him an unfortunately clinical view of Becky's backside. "Don't fart. You'll scare him off."

Becky's response was a sharp jab to his kidney. "Put me down."

"I don't want to hurt the bee. Let me swat him first." Duncan tentatively moved a hand toward her lower half.

He earned a second kidney shot before he could even make contact. "Um, fuck no. Just set me on my legs."

Duncan complied, sliding her weight off his shoulders. The bee flapped its iridescent wings and relocated, landing a few inches away on her right butt cheek. It lingered there, seemingly content to watch the world—until it unceremoniously buried its stinger deep into her flesh.

-10 HP

"YEAAOCH!" Becky's scream tore through the quiet field.

Her hand came down with the practiced ferocity of a stripper removing an unwanted hand from her body. SMACK. The impact sent a visible ripple through her leggings as she slapped her own skin. "Fuck! Fuck! That hurts!"

Duncan doubled over, the laughter finally breaking through his fatigue. "Should've let me swat it."

Becky sneered at him, her face twisting into a "mean drunk" mask as her cheek began to swell. Limping slightly, the two continued their pathetic trek toward the Comic Sans promise of Mellonwood.

Part 4

The dot on the horizon finally expanded into a settlement, though "settlement" was a generous term for the visual assault that awaited them. Strung across the entrance was a banner featuring Pink Comic Sans lettering on a vibrating purple background.

"Who the fuck made that? A kindergartner on a sugar crash?" Duncan's nerd rage spiked as he wrestled the Malibu bottle away from Becky for another tactical chug.

"I like it," Becky slurred, her eyes glazed as she admired the neon atrocity. She was watching the way the pink light bled into the purple background, creating a neon haze that felt like a warm hug for her overstimulated brain.

"You would." Duncan slapped his palm against his forehead with a wet thud.

"What the hell is that supposed to mean?" Becky pouted; she felt the old, familiar sting—the "you're too stupid to get it" tone she'd heard from every teacher and every boyfriend. "It's just pretty okay!" She flung back her arm to give Duncan another kidney ache with her fist.

Duncan flinched with the grace of a Level 3 Warrior who was secretly terrified of a 110-pound brunette. "Hey! Stop it! We're on the same team, remember? Quest objectives and all that shit?"

Becky didn't punch him. Instead, she stuck her finger in her mouth, slobbered on it with focused intensity, and jammed it deep into Duncan's ear canal.

"A fucking wet-willy? Jesus!" Duncan recoiled, reaching out to return the favor. But Becky was already gone, giggling as she scrambled under the banner and vanished into the haze of the town square.

Duncan lunged after her, but physics and his own mass proved to be his greatest enemies. His ankle buckled under his 280-pound frame with a sickening pop.

-20 HP. SHOULD HAVE JUST LET HER HIT YOU.

"Fuck you, AI!" Duncan snarled, clutching the throbbing joint as he hobbled toward the town square.

Mellonwood was an architectural insult—the visual manifestation of an architect's absolute laziness. The buildings weren't made of stone or timber; they were oversized cardboard boxes haphazardly drawn on with Crayon. The NPCs stood like emotionless droids, their expressions vacant and flat, draped in robes of dull pink and oppressive purple.

"This place is so fucking stupid," Duncan muttered, his dreams of epic castles replaced by a preschool-themed nightmare populated by grumpy townsfolk.

Suddenly, a ping echoed in his UI as a mini-map materialized. "Oh, thank the gods."

He hovered over a shimmering green circle: QUEST OBJECTIVE: FIND THE TAVERN OWNER.

His 1,034 days, 18 hours, and 37 seconds of World of Warcraft experience kicked in—the green dot brought him to the completion of the quest. With a swollen ankle and a heart full of resentment, he limped off to find Becky—who was no doubt nursing her bee-stung backside—so they could finally get obliterated at the pub.
