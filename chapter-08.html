Chapter 8: Now Rest Friends

Part 1

"Uh… okay."

Duncan squinted at the quest text, but the words refused to line up. A deep yawn split his face. He turned, swaying slightly, and shuffled back into the tavern.

"Got a quest," he muttered. "But I'm so damned sleepy."

Vern smiled, the kind of smile that didn't rush anything.

"Then rest, my friend."

He gestured toward a side room. The door swung open on its own. Inside waited a narrow twin bed—plain, clean, and impossibly inviting.

Duncan didn't hesitate. He crossed the room, collapsed onto the mattress, and was asleep before his boots stopped shifting.

The breathalyzer chimed.

Duncan didn't move.

Didn't stir.

He was gone.

Becky drained the last of her martini and set the glass down. "So… what happens if he can't blow?"

"It stops," Vern said, already wiping the glass clean. "Then it tries again in thirty minutes."

He glanced at her. "Another?"

Becky hesitated. The room felt quieter now. Heavier.

"No," she said finally. "I'm tired too."

Vern nodded and waved her toward another door.

Duncan began to dream.

He was—

—handsome?

No, not just handsome. Effortlessly handsome. The kind of handsome that required no lighting or forgiveness.

He was a warlock. Just like Boxxy.

A glorious title hovered above his head in gold, gently pulsing:

MASTER PANTY DROPPER — DUNCAN

Meowth Vader perched proudly on his shoulder like a demonic familiar who had never once missed a meal. In dreams, the cat could talk.

"Meow," Vader purred. "I am so happy. The popsicles. The fish ones. More of those."

"Same, buddy," Duncan said, nodding sagely. "Same."

They bumped foreheads.

They strolled through a medieval city fused with steampunk brass and neon signage. Gear-driven streetlights hummed. Pipes hissed. The cobblestones were smooth, incredibly walkable.

Women noticed.

They always noticed.

"Awww," a group of voluptuous women sighed as they passed. "Cute cat."

"And an even cuter cat dad," another added, biting her lip like it was a paid endorsement.

Duncan stopped, turned, and executed an exaggerated bow. The title above his head sparkled harder.

Meowth Vader leapt down and immediately head-butted the nearest blonde woman in the ankle.

She gasped. "Oh my god, he's so squishy."

She squatted to pet him, giving Duncan a generous, slow-motion view of her upper body.

Vader looked up at Duncan. "Meow. You're welcome."

Vader winked.

Life was perfect.

Then came the sound.

Not loud.

Not sudden.

A warning.

A low, distant roar rolled through the city, deep enough to rattle the pipes beneath the streets.

The neon flickered.

One sign buzzed, then went dark.

The ground began to shake.

The cobblestones cracked, glowing lines snapping into jagged fractures. Steam hissed violently from broken vents. The women vanished—not running, not screaming—just gone.

Duncan's smile faded.

"Hey," he muttered. "This part wasn't in the fantasy."

A building screamed as its frame twisted, iron tearing free from stone. High above, a tower began to fold inward, collapsing directly toward Meowth Vader.

"Vader!" Duncan shouted.

He ran.

The title above his head was gone.

The neon missing.

Duncan launched himself forward, grabbing the cat just as the shadow of the falling structure swallowed them—

BEEP. BEEP.

Duncan SLAMMED awake—

—lungs burning, heart hammering, hands clutching at nothing because Vader wasn't there, the cat was GONE, he'd failed, he'd FAILED—

"Holy shit."

He was in the tavern room.

Narrow bed. Plain walls. Breathalyzer shrieking in his pocket.

Not a collapsing city.

Not a nightmare.

Just: awake.

He blew.

0.105.

"Oh, fuck, oh, fuck, I need a drink now!"

Duncan ran out of the room. No Vern. He hopped behind the bar, grabbed the nearest bottle, and chugged.

Part 2

"How long was I out?"

Duncan squinted at the darkened room. The tavern was quiet. Becky was still asleep. Whatever passed for night in this place hadn't lifted yet.

He pulled up the quest timer.

KEEP DUNCAN SOBER (ISH) 17:57:20

He frowned. "Four hours."

His BAC had dropped from 0.231 to 0.105 in roughly four and a half hours. That wasn't normal. Not even close.

Duncan opened the calculator app and tapped in the numbers, lips moving as he worked it out.

"Point one two six over four and a half… zero point zero three an hour." He swallowed. "Too fast. Way too fucking fast."

He guzzled more alcohol and lingered at the bar, elbows planted, eyes burning for sleep.

"The fuck," he muttered. "I can't lose my baby."

His gaze drifted around the room. Vern's mantle loomed over the fireplace—a boar's head mounted above it, glass eyes catching the firelight. The flames were still going strong. Duncan shuffled over, tossed another log onto the fire, and collapsed into a chair in the lounge.

Becky's snoring droned faintly in the background.

His head dipped.

Just for a second.

BEEP. BEEP.

The breathalyzer shrieked.

"Turn that shit off!" Becky yelled from her room.

Duncan jolted upright, fumbling for the device, hands shaking as he blew.

0.165.

He froze, staring at the display.

"Yeah. Nope."

He shoved the device into his pocket. Passing out again wasn't an option. The margin was razor-thin, and the game clearly wasn't bluffing.

"What now?"

He grabbed a napkin and scrawled PUT IT ON MY TAB, staggered back to the bar, slapped it down where Vern would see it, and seized another bottle of Dragon's Piss vodka before retreating to his room.

Back on the bed, he pulled out his phone.

Stats first. He was about 300 XP shy of the next level—close enough to taste. He reread his class description, letting the mechanics sink in.

Rage thresholds.

Ability costs.

Stacking Charge into Tackle for crowd control.

Becky was basically pure DPS now. He'd have to compensate.

The absurdity of the game unsettled him—but once he saw the rules, it all made sense. This was systems. This was optimization.

This was home.

One app caught his eye: Party Inspection.

He took another swig and tapped Becky's profile. A 3D model of her rotated slowly on-screen, fully geared.

He exhaled. "Probably the closest thing to porn in here."

Pfft. Pffft. Flurrrt.

Duncan gagged and then muttered, "Fuck no. Not Becky. Never Becky."

He glanced back at the app. Her XP bar was climbing at the same pace as his.

Duncan leaned back against the headboard, exhaled slowly, and stared at the ceiling.

"At least," he murmured, "I'm not leveling alone."

He drank half the bottle—on purpose this time—and let sleep take him.

Part 3

For whatever reason, Duncan's ears had become attuned to the breathalyzer's beep. When it sounded, he stirred automatically—half-awake—blew into the device, then took a drink to keep his BAC within the safe zone.

You couldn't call it restorative sleep. Alcohol rarely gave you that on its own, let alone when it dragged you awake every thirty minutes like a cruel alarm clock.

Duncan drank a lot.

But never like this.

This was excessive—even for him.

He drifted in and out of consciousness, vaguely aware of Vern moving through the tavern, Becky getting up, sunlight creeping through the windows. When he finally focused, he checked the quest timer.

KEEP DUNCAN SOBER (ISH) 13:57:20

He blew into the breathalyzer.

0.184.

Still safe.

"Becky," Duncan called from his room. "I got a new quest."

"Oh, yeah?" Becky leaned into the doorway, unimpressed.

"It's to gather Life Berries. Supposed to help the goblins make healing potions."

Duncan threw off his blanket, scratched his balls, and shuffled out into the common room.

"Ugh," she said, making a gagging motion with her finger and sticking out her tongue. "Buddy. Keep your hands out of your pants."

Becky rolled her eyes hard enough to strain her optic nerve, then flagged Vern for breakfast and ordered a spicy Bloody Mary with it.

"Seriously?" Duncan said. "I thought we were getting along."

"You clearly thought wrong." She took a sip, then chased it with celery.

"Now, children," Vern said, emerging from the back with two plates. "You gotta work together."

He set the food down on the bar. Steam rose from it—hot, fresh, real.

"Scrambled drake eggs. Tuskling blood sausage. Flapjacks."

Duncan sat immediately and started inhaling his meal.

SLOW DOWN. DON'T CHOKE. MEOWTH VADER NEEDS A FATHER.

Duncan paused just long enough to flip off the ceiling, then kept eating.

"Ew," Becky said, wrinkling her nose. "I'm not eating this stuff."

"It's epic. You gotta have some," Duncan said through a mouthful of food. A smear of drake egg clung to the corner of his beard.

"It's got eighty grams of protein," Vern added. "All the essential nutrients too. You're gonna need the energy."

Becky eyed the plate suspiciously. She picked up a piece of tuskling blood sausage, lifted it to her nose, and sniffed it like a curious cat.

Then she licked it.

"…I guess I've had worse things in my mouth."

Duncan snorted.

Becky froze.

"…What—?" She blinked, then groaned. "Okay. Egg boy."

EAT YOUR DAMN BREAKFAST, BECKY. I'M GETTING BORED.

"Right, AI," Becky said flatly. "I'm not doing anything. I'm gonna sit here and drink all day."

She leaned back on her stool in defiance.

POOF.

A bracelet snapped into existence around Becky's wrist.

Another appeared on Duncan's.

An ethereal chain unfurled between them, glowing faintly as it locked into place.

ACHIEVEMENT UNLOCKED: BUTT BUDDIES
You are now linked. If one of you dies, the other dies too.
NOW GET ON WITH THE QUESTING.

Duncan stared down at the chain.

"…You had to say something."
